# Director's shot vocabulary

Read this in Phase B while building the storyboard. The point of a storyboard is *variety that cuts together* — a sequence where each shot does a different job and the differences create rhythm. A set of six hero shots at the same ¾ eye-level is a contact sheet, not a film.

Use this as a menu, not a checklist. Pick the shots the *idea* needs; don't force one of each.

## Shot types (what the frame is doing)

| Shot | What it's for | When it earns its place |
|---|---|---|
| **Wide / establishing** | Place the hero in its world; set the promise | Open on it — the viewer needs to know where they are and what they're looking at before you go close |
| **Hero / portrait** | The product as icon, full and proud | The lock-up; or a beat that's purely about desirability |
| **Push-in / pull-out** | Direct attention; build or release tension | Moving from world → product (push) or product → result (pull) |
| **Low / heroic angle** | Make the product feel powerful, monumental | When the beat is about capability/strength |
| **High / top-down** | Show a surface and the action on it clearly | Surfaces seen from above read instantly (a floor, a bed, a table) |
| **Macro / detail** | Prove the mechanism — the thing actually working | The "how it works" beat: the roller lifting debris, the brush gathering, the seal sealing |
| **Cutaway / result** | Pay off the action — before/after, the clean outcome | Right after a working beat; it's the proof |
| **Two-up / context** | Product + the surface/scenario together | When the *relationship* (tool ↔ surface) is the point |

## Camera moves (how the still animates downstream)

These are notes *for the editor* — this skill renders stills; the video-editor adds the motion (Remotion on stills, no generative video at v1). Specify the intended move so the storyboard reads as motion:

- **Push-in / dolly** — slow scale-up toward the subject; the workhorse for "look here."
- **Parallax** — foreground and background drift at different rates; gives a flat still depth.
- **Element drift** — debris pulled toward the head, light raking across, a path travelling through frame.
- **Cross-dissolve / match-cut** — soften a cut, or hard-cut on a shared shape (the product body) so the *tool/surface* swaps while the hero stays put. The match-cut on the product is the cleanest way to say "same machine, different job."
- **Settle / hold** — the lock-up; motion comes to rest with room for the super.

## Angle variety — the cheap win

If every beat is shot at the same height and distance, the film feels static even with motion on top. The fastest way to make a sequence feel directed is to **change the camera's relationship to the subject between beats**: a wide establish → a low heroic push → a tight macro on the action → a high-angle cutaway to the result reads as a *crafted* sequence even though the subject never changed. Bake the angle into each beat's render prompt (Phase C) so the vibe KV already carries it.

## Rhythm

- **Open wide, end on the hero/lock-up.** Bookends orient the viewer and leave them with the product + message.
- **Alternate scale.** Don't stack two wides or two macros back to back; let the cut breathe by changing distance.
- **One idea per beat.** A sub-second cut can land exactly one thing — one tool, one surface, one outcome. Versatility is shown *across* beats, never crammed into one frame.
- **Let the connective motif do the talking.** Whatever changes on each cut (the tool, the room, the angle) IS the argument; pick it deliberately and keep it consistent.
