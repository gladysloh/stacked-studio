# Example prompts

All of these stay within the word ceiling, open with the camera, name the product without describing it, and end with only the negatives they need. The brand in them, Examplia, is invented.

## 1. No style reference: full structure (4:5, follow brand)
> Low angle, medium close-up, three-quarter, product on the right third, standing upright — editorial skincare photography. The Examplia Cloud Serum bottle on a pale white-oak bathroom shelf, a folded linen towel softly out of focus on the left, light steam on the mirror behind. Soft directional window light from camera right, warm golden bias, feathered low-contrast shadows, a gentle contact shadow under the bottle. Palette: bone white, warm oak, muted sage accent (#9DB4A0). Upper third left as calm negative space. Complete product shown from base to cap, nothing cropped. The only text visible is printed on the product packaging. Calm, clinical, warm, reassuring, unhurried.

## 2. Style reference attached: terse (1:1)
> Eye level, close-up, straight-on, centre-frame. The Examplia Cloud Serum bottle standing on wet slate, scattered water droplets as in the reference. Environment: dark seamless, slate edge catching light. Lighting: hard single side light from camera left, crisp specular highlights on the droplets. Mood: austere, precise, cool, quiet, premium.

## 3. Several products, named by visual handle (16:9)
> Eye level, wide shot, frontal. Three products in a shallow arc on travertine, left to right: the tall amber dropper bottle, the squat white jar with the gold lid, the slim frosted tube. All upright, evenly spaced, fully visible. Right third: soft out-of-focus travertine wall as copy space. Large softbox from above left, soft shadows, gentle reflection in the stone. Warm editorial grade. Considered, warm, tactile, luminous, curated.

## 4. Revision from feedback (same concept, fix one thing)
Previous prompt kept as is. Feedback: "bottle looks like it's floating". The only change is added to the subject line:
> …the bottle standing upright on the shelf with a soft contact shadow directly beneath it, base clearly resting on the wood.

## 5. Small relative change (when the model keeps snapping back)
> Match the previous frame's composition, palette and lighting. The only change: the camera is about 10° lower than in the previous frame, same direction, so slightly more of the bottle's base is visible. Do not change the product's rotation.

Build each step on the latest result, not the original. If three small steps don't move it, change approach (a new reference, a different angle, compositing).
