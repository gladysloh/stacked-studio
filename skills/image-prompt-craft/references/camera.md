# Camera

Open the prompt with the camera, one term per axis: **height + distance + orientation**. The model weights early words most, and a camera term at the end gets ignored. An explicit camera term beats a style reference's angle, though the reference still controls the product and the look.

## Height (where the camera sits)
| Term | Effect |
|---|---|
| overhead / top-down (90°) | graphic, flat, no perspective; flat lays |
| high angle | looking down; shows the surface and the arrangement |
| 45° tabletop | the classic product angle; top and front both read |
| eye level | neutral, honest |
| low angle | looking up; the product feels heroic and monumental |
| ground level / worm's-eye | extreme scale; needs an anchor (see below) |

## Distance (how much fills the frame)
extreme close-up (texture, a detail) · close-up · medium close-up · medium shot · wide shot (whole object plus surroundings) · long / establishing (environment dominates, subject small)

## Orientation (which face the subject shows)
straight-on / frontal symmetrical · three-quarter (about 45°) · profile (90°) · back three-quarter · dutch tilt (rotated horizon for tension; use rarely)

## Position in frame
centre-frame · left third · right third · lower third · off-axis left/right. Pair this with [composition.md](composition.md).

## Lens: describe the visible result, not the gear
| Say | Not |
|---|---|
| "compressed background, shallow depth of field, creamy bokeh" | "85mm f/1.4" |
| "natural perspective, minimal distortion" | "50mm" |
| "macro: surface texture readable in fine detail" | "100mm macro" |
| "expanded space, exaggerated foreground" (environments only; it distorts products) | "16mm" |

Depth of field: shallow (one plane sharp, background dissolves), moderate (product sharp, background soft), or deep (everything sharp, typical for flat lays).

## True stance, kept separate from camera tilt
State how the product actually stands ("standing upright on the shelf", "laid flat", "tilted back 15° against the stone"). A perspective tilt and a real rotation look alike to the model; mixing them produces floating or leaning products.

## Making a strong angle stick
The model drifts back to eye level unless the angle is anchored three ways:
1. **Position plus tilt:** "camera below the product, looking upward".
2. **A verification phrase** the model can check:
   - low → "with the bottom of the bottle clearly visible";
   - top-down → "with the lid's full top surface visible";
   - macro → "with surface texture readable in fine detail".
3. **A counter-default:** "correct proportions, no fisheye warping, no curved verticals".

A setup analogy can help if you decouple it from the literal surface: "as if shot through glass from below, but without any glass present".

**Dramatic angles need a visual anchor** (a surface, a horizon, a contact shadow), or the geometry collapses.

## Across variants and shots
Change the camera deliberately between variants or storyboard beats: a wide establishing shot, then a low heroic shot, then a macro reads as directed. Six eye-level three-quarter shots read as a contact sheet.

## Example openers
- "Low angle, medium close-up, three-quarter, product right third, standing upright — "
- "Overhead top-down, wide, flat lay, products evenly spaced — "
- "Eye level, close-up, straight-on, centre-frame, shallow depth of field — "
