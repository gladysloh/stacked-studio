# Composition

Composition decides where the eye goes. With a style reference attached, leave composition to the reference unless the direction changes it. Without one, state it explicitly.

## Placement and balance
- **Centred and dominant:** iconic, hero, lock-up.
- **Centred and small:** the product surrounded by space or environment; premium, calm.
- **Offset (thirds):** dynamic, leaves room for copy.
- **Deliberate crop:** energy and scale. Never crop the product itself unless the direction asks; say "complete product shown from base to lid, nothing cropped".
- **Symmetric** (formal, graphic) vs **asymmetric** (tension, movement).

## Negative space and copy space
Generous space reads premium; tight framing reads busy and energetic. When a layout needs room for a headline, **name where the space is**. Ask for empty space only; the copy itself is added later in layout, never rendered:
- "Upper half of frame: deep, clean negative space for headline copy."
- "Left third empty soft-grey wall; product on the right third."

## Depth
- **Flat:** everything on one plane; graphic, flat lay.
- **Layered:** foreground, product, background. A soft out-of-focus foreground element adds depth, but keep it off the product and its label.

## Compose for the real format
| Format | Composes well as |
|---|---|
| 1:1 | centred hero, or a balanced flat lay |
| 4:5 | single vertical hero; slight headroom |
| 9:16 | vertical stack; keep critical content in the middle band (roughly y250–1600 of 1920), because the top and bottom sit under platform UI |
| 16:9 | horizontal split: product on one third, environment or copy space on the other |

Side-by-side subjects want landscape; a single tall subject wants portrait. Never describe a composition that fights the frame. For moving a finished image into other formats, see [format-adaptation](../../format-adaptation/SKILL.md).

## Several products
- Up to about 3 products compose reliably in one pass. Give each a position ("left, slightly behind the centre jar").
- Name each by a short **visual handle** ("the gold tube with the brown cap"), not "image 1".
- Strict front-to-back order and 5+ products are unreliable. Use compositing (background pass, then a colour-matched product pass); see [image rendering](../../../docs/contributing/image-rendering.md).

## Props
Few, on-brief, never competing with the product for attention. Say how they're arranged: "scattered loosely", "arranged precisely", or "framing the product". See [surface-and-material.md](surface-and-material.md).
